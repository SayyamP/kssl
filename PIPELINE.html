From a news page to a card on the screen

Every number the KSSL dashboard shows is produced by the chain below, on this machine, from documents the crawler fetched. This page explains that chain in plain English — what each step reads, what it writes, and which database column it lands in. It is generated from the running code and the live database, so it cannot drift from what actually happens.

What this is

The dashboard has three pillars (Competitive, Market, Technology) and a set of tabs under each. Nothing on those tabs is typed by a person. A document is fetched, read by two local models, filtered by rules, judged by a local model, checked against its own evidence, and only then written to the tables the screen reads.

The rule the whole design serves: the screen may only show something the source actually says. Where the corpus cannot support a number, the tab stays empty rather than filled with a plausible one.

Two schemas

extracted is what the readers produced — documents, spans, propositions, entities. serving is what the screen reads. Nothing skips from one to the other without passing a gate.

Two origins

Every serving row carries origin: reference (the hand-researched demo seed, now archived) or pipeline (produced by this chain). The API serves only pipeline.

Local models only

A 7B extractor plus GLiNER read the documents; a 14B judge writes and verifies the cards. Both run on this machine through Ollama. No text leaves the box.

The whole path

Read it as a funnel. Thousands of sentences become hundreds of spans, spans become propositions with quotes, propositions survive a series of refusals, and the few that survive become a row on a tab. Every stage is allowed to say no, and every no is counted and printed.

1 · The raw document

A document enters as fetched text — a press release, a news article, a procurement notice. It is stored whole, in its original language, with the URL it came from. The text is never rewritten: everything downstream quotes it by character offset, so if the stored text changed, every quote would become a lie.

2 · Layer A — reading the document

Layer A is the comprehension engine (a 7B model plus GLiNER, run per chunk). It does two things, and both are anchored to the text.

Spans. It marks every meaningful stretch of text and says what kind of thing it is — an organisation, a weapon system, a country, a date, a money value — and what it means in English (gloss), even when the document is Swedish or Persian. Each span records the exact character positions it covers.

Propositions. It then states what the document says, as subject – predicate – object, each one carrying the sentence it came from as a verbatim quote plus that quote's offsets. This is the unit everything later reads. No later step ever sees the raw page again — only these grounded statements.

3 · Layer B — who is who

The same company is written differently everywhere — Bharat Forge, Bharat Forge Limited, Kalyani. Layer B resolves mentions into canonical entities with their aliases, so counting, ordering and de-duplication all work on one identity rather than three.

A second, smaller identity layer (pipeline/aliases.py) sits in front of the serving side. It folds legal suffixes (Ltd, GmbH, S.A.), maps known variants (Hanwha Defense → Hanwha Aerospace), and encodes one standing rule: Kalyani, KSSL and Bharat Forge are one client identity — the client group, never a rival, never a threat to itself.

4 · The gates (before any LLM)

Most documents never reach a model. The cheap refusals run first, in this order, and each one is counted and printed at the end of every run — a skipped document is a reported number, never a silent disappearance.

audit suppressiondocument ids an audit ruled out a human verdict must outlive the next re-run

Two of these gates exist because they failed once in production. An August-2026 news query returned a July-2024 article and the dashboard showed it as current — so recency is now proved per article, from the article's own dates, in thirteen languages. And a “document” that was really a site's headline ticker produced a card whose title, summary and quotes came from three unrelated stories — so listing pages are refused by URL shape before anything reads them.

5 · Signals: competitive / market / technology

This is the step that fills the three Overview feeds. For one document that passed every gate, the judge model is shown only that document's propositions with their quotes — never the raw page — and asked one question: is there a signal here a KSSL analyst should see, and which pillar does it belong to?

Competitive

A rival's move: an order won, a partnership, an expansion, a product launch that changes KSSL's field. KSSL's own news is never a competitive signal — the pillar tracks rivals.

Market

A demand event: a tender, a government order, a budget, an import or export decision that changes what buyers want.

Technology

A capability advance: a system demonstrated, a technical milestone, an innovation that moves what is expected in a KSSL category.

The prompt, exactly as the code sends it

The %s placeholders are filled in at call time with the nine KSSL categories, then the document's title, source, language, and its list of statements.

6 · The entailment check

Grounding is not entailment. The extractor guarantees the quotes are real; it does not guarantee the card built on them is what those quotes say. So every card is shown back to the model with its own evidence and asked whether the evidence supports it. Cards that fail are deleted and the reason is printed.

You are verifying a competitive-intelligence card against its evidence.

CARD CLAIM:
Title: %s
Why it matters: %s

EVIDENCE (verbatim quotes from the source article):
%s

Question: do the quotes SUPPORT the card? Judge in two parts:
1. The TITLE's facts (who, what, numbers) must be stated or directly implied by
   the quotes -- not merely the same company or topic. A title that upgrades the
   claim (announced -> delivered, plan -> contract, talks -> order, concept ->
   product) is NOT supported.
2. The why-it-matters is an analyst's reading; it may INFER significance from
   those facts. Reject it only if it asserts facts the quotes contradict or
   do not contain at all.
Reply with exactly one line:
SUPPORTED
or
UNSUPPORTED: <one short reason>

The check is deliberately asymmetric: the facts in the title must be stated or directly implied (announced is not delivered, a plan is not a contract, talks are not an order), while the why-it-matters may infer significance — it is refused only if it asserts something the quotes do not contain. An unparseable verdict never deletes a card.

8 · Tenders (live APIs, not news)

News reports awards; it rarely publishes an open solicitation with a deadline and a value. So the Tender Pipeline is fed from procurement APIs directly — SAM.gov (US), TED (EU, by defence CPV codes), and India's GeM and CPPP — filtered to KSSL's portfolio and mapped onto the same nine categories.

sourcehow it is queriedwhat is kept
SAM.gov (US)opportunities API, date-window slicing, ten rotating keys read from the TendPro environment at runtimenotices whose title matches the portfolio
TED (EU)search API restricted to defence CPV codes — weapons and ammunition, military vehicles, warships, military aircraft and UAVs English titles where the notice provides them
GeM (India)public bid-search API with the Ministry of Defence filter defence bids in KSSL categories
CPPP (India)the captcha-free pagers nothing, in the current window — those feeds are dominated by civil works; deeper defence rows need the captcha-gated search

A tender is kept only if its title matches the portfolio; issuer names were disqualified as evidence after a company called “Armoured Vehicles Nigam” filed floor-tile tenders that the filter read as armoured vehicles. Bare words that look military but are not (“cartridge” in cartridge-type heater, “armored” in Armored Brigade turf replacement) are excluded by name.

Two writers share this table — the API fetcher (ids prefixed sam_, ted_, gem_) and the news step (numeric ids). Each deletes only its own id space; a blanket delete wiped the API feed once, which is why the boundary is now explicit.
